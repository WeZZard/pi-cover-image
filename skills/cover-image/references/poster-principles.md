# Poster Principles

Principles for a cover image that earns its place on an article. The `idea-extractor` reads this before it extracts anything, so extraction serves the cover, not the abstract.

## What a cover is for

- A cover earns the click; it does not recap the argument. It is a hook, not a summary.
- The reader sees it for half a second at thumbnail size before reading a word. If it needs the title to make sense, it has already failed.

## Six tests a cover must pass

1. **Atmosphere fit.** The cover's energy matches the article's register. An analytical piece wants restraint and precision; a polemic wants boldness; a narrative wants a scene; a tutorial wants clarity. A dramatic image on a measured paper is a lie about the paper — it tells the reader the article is something it is not.
2. **Thumbnail legibility.** It reduces to three masses and still reads. Detail is a bonus, not the message.
3. **Freshness.** No cliché native to the device (no lightbulb for ideas, no chain for freedom, no brain for AI, no gears for systems). If the device's obvious image is the first one you thought of, discard it and find a second.
4. **Specificity.** A cover that could label any article in the field is worthless. It must point at THIS article's turn, not the topic.
5. **One read.** One focal point, one relationship. Two ideas compete and neither survives the thumbnail.
6. **Title link.** The reader meets the cover and the title as a pair, before reading a word. The pair must visibly belong together: a reader who has not read the article can say why THIS image sits next to THIS title. Run the swap test — if the image would fit a different title just as well, the link has failed, however specific the image is to the article's content. Specificity is judged from the article's side; the title link is judged from the reader's side, and the reader holds only the title's words.

## Where the idea comes from

The idea is not always the core argument. It can be:

- the article's central tension,
- its mechanism (how the thing actually works),
- its telling example,
- its surprise (the thing the reader did not expect),
- its mood or field,

whichever single thing a cover can turn into a glance. Pick the one that makes the best cover, not the one that is most "the thesis." The center word of the article is the hero of the visual, not a side element.

## Scoring

When several idea+rhetoric candidates are on the table, score each 0–5 on the six tests above and sum to 30. Keep the highest. Ties break toward atmosphere fit, then title link, then freshness. Reject any candidate whose image energy contradicts the article's register, no matter how well it maps the argument — a perfect mapping in the wrong register is a bad cover. The title-link score is the extractor's self-estimate; an independent `title-link-reflector` re-judges it downstream and may send candidates back.
